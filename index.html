1. Scope and Users

This Privacy Policy applies to the following categories of users:

  • Providers (Therapists): Licensed clinicians who use the Service to receive case assignments and deliver in-home visits.
  • Service Coordinators and Managers: Operations and administrative personnel who create patient charts, assign cases, manage provider accounts, and oversee operations.
  • Patients: Individuals receiving therapy services. Patients do not directly access the Service in the current version; information about patients is entered and managed by Service Coordinators on behalf of the patient’s treatment.

If you are a patient, our practices regarding your PHI are described in detail in our Notice of Privacy Practices, which we are required to provide under HIPAA. This Privacy Policy supplements but does not replace the Notice of Privacy Practices.

2. Information We Collect

2.1 Information You or Your Coordinator Provides

Provider account information:

  • Full name, email address, and phone number
  • Gender
  • Zip codes and geographic areas covered
  • Availability (days, times, locations served)
  • Rate (set and editable by Manager only)
  • Credentialed insurances (set and editable by Manager only)
  • Professional license number and credentialing documentation, where applicable
  • Tax identification information needed for payment, where applicable (treated as confidential and stored separately from PHI)

Patient information (entered by Service Coordinator on the patient’s behalf):

  • Full name, home address, zip code, and contact information
  • Insurance carrier, member identifier, or self-pay status
  • Copayment, deductible, and self-pay amounts
  • Requested visit days and times
  • Gender preference for provider, if any
  • Clinical documentation associated with each visit, including precautions, home exercise plans, follow-up records, and any other information relevant to the patient’s care
  • Payment status and method (payment link or cash receipt)

2.2 Information Collected Automatically During Use

Visit lifecycle data: Check-in and check-out times, session duration, visit outcome (completed, no-answer escalated, etc.), payment selection, and follow-up scheduling status.

Door verification photographs. When a patient does not answer the door at a scheduled visit and the Service Coordinator is unable to reach the patient by phone within the 15-minute escalation window, the Provider is required by the Service to capture a photograph of the patient’s door using the in-app camera. This photograph documents the attempted visit for compliance, billing-dispute, and quality-assurance purposes.

  • Photographs in this flow may be captured only through the in-app live camera; selection from the device photo gallery is not permitted, and the gallery selection feature is disabled.
  • Before capture, the Service displays an on-screen warning advising the Provider that photographing anything other than the patient’s door may constitute trespassing, an invasion of privacy, or another legal violation. Providers must comply with this restriction and with all applicable federal, state, and local laws.
  • Incidental information. A door photograph may incidentally capture identifying information such as a door number, name plate, mat, decoration, or a person passing in the hallway. We treat any such incidental information as confidential and apply the same access restrictions as we apply to PHI.
  • Storage and access. Door verification photographs are stored as part of the patient’s visit record. Access is restricted to the assigned Service Coordinator, the Manager, and the originating Provider. Photographs are retained in accordance with Section 9 (Data Retention).
  • If you believe a photograph captured you or your property in error, contact us using the details in Section 20. We will investigate, restrict access, and, where appropriate, delete the image, subject to legal hold and medical-records retention requirements.

Audit log data: Every action that affects a record — check-in, check-out, no-answer escalation, bid submission, bid acceptance, rate change, availability change, and similar events — is recorded with the acting user’s identifier, a timestamp, and (where applicable) the prior value of the field that changed. Audit logs are required under HIPAA Security Rule § 164.312(b).

Device location. The Provider’s approximate or precise device location may be collected only at the moment of check-in, to verify proximity to the scheduled visit address. We do not collect continuous, background, or off-visit location data. If proximity verification is not enabled, no location data is collected. Where the Provider declines location permission, alternate check-in confirmation may be required.

Payment data:

  • Card and bank payments are processed by our third-party payment processor, QuickBooks. Full card numbers and bank account numbers are not stored on our systems. We receive only confirmation tokens, last-four digits, and transaction metadata sufficient to reconcile the visit record. QuickBooks is responsible for PCI-DSS compliance with respect to the data it handles, and we maintain a Business Associate Agreement with QuickBooks where required.
  • Cash collections are recorded by the Provider as a confirmation entry. We do not transmit cash to any third party; we record only the fact, amount, and date of the cash collection in the visit record.

Technical and diagnostic data: Device type, operating system version, app version, IP address, language and locale settings, crash logs, and error reports. We use this data to maintain reliability and security, and not for advertising.

2.3 Information We Do Not Collect

We do not collect contacts, browsing history, microphone audio, advertising identifiers, behavioral profiles, or any data used for targeted advertising. The Service contains no third-party advertising and no advertising-related trackers.

3. How We Use Information

We use the information we collect for the following purposes, consistent with HIPAA-permitted uses for Treatment, Payment, and Health Care Operations, and consistent with applicable state law:

  • Treatment: Matching patients with eligible providers; coordinating visits; documenting clinical encounters; transmitting precautions and home exercises.
  • Payment: Verifying insurance eligibility; processing copayments, deductibles, and self-pay amounts; reconciling cash collections; managing the bid pool for unmatched cases.
  • Healthcare operations: Quality assurance; provider credentialing; auditing; staff training; risk management; and operational analytics relating to case funnel, visit outcomes, SLA compliance, and bid pool activity.
  • Compliance and legal: Maintaining audit logs; responding to lawful requests; complying with HIPAA, state medical privacy laws, and other applicable laws.
  • Fraud prevention and security: Detecting and investigating suspected misuse of the Service, including suspected misuse of the door verification feature.
  • Service reliability: Diagnosing and resolving technical issues, monitoring performance, and improving usability.

We do not use PHI for marketing or sell PHI, except as expressly permitted by HIPAA and after obtaining any authorization required by law.

5. Sale and Sharing of Personal Information

We do not sell personal information for monetary or other valuable consideration, and we do not share personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising, as those terms are defined under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA), or under similar state laws. We have not done so in the preceding 12 months.

6. Security

We implement administrative, physical, and technical safeguards designed to meet the requirements of the HIPAA Security Rule (45 C.F.R. Part 164, Subpart C) and other applicable security standards. These include:

  • Encryption of data in transit using industry-standard TLS, and encryption of data at rest.
  • Role-based access controls limiting access to PHI to the minimum necessary for each user’s role.
  • Mandatory authentication for every account; audit logging of every access and modification.
  • Restriction of the in-app camera flow for door verification to live capture, with gallery uploads disabled.
  • Workforce training on HIPAA Privacy and Security obligations.
  • Regular risk analysis and risk management consistent with 45 C.F.R. § 164.308(a)(1).
  • Documented contingency planning, including backup, disaster recovery, and emergency mode operation procedures.

No security program is perfect. We work continuously to protect information, but we cannot guarantee absolute security against every possible threat.

7. User Device Security Requirements

Users are responsible for maintaining reasonable security safeguards on any device used to access the Service. Such safeguards include, but are not limited to, maintaining device passcodes or biometric authentication, installing operating system and security updates, preventing unauthorized access, and immediately reporting lost, stolen, compromised, or unauthorized devices to the Company. Failure to comply with these requirements may result in suspension or restriction of access to the Service.

8. Breach Notification

In the event of a breach of unsecured PHI, we will provide notification consistent with the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule (45 C.F.R. Part 164, Subpart D) and applicable state breach-notification laws, including:

  • Notification to affected individuals without unreasonable delay and in no case later than 60 days after discovery of the breach.
  • Notification to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services in accordance with HIPAA requirements.
  • Notification to prominent media outlets where required by law (breaches affecting more than 500 residents of a state or jurisdiction).
  • Where Physical Therapy of the City P.C., d/b/a Home Recovery Therapy Services is acting as a Business Associate, prompt notification to the affected Covered Entity in accordance with our Business Associate Agreement.

9. Data Retention

We retain information for as long as needed to provide the Service and comply with our legal obligations:

  • Patient health records, visit documentation, and door verification photographs are retained for the period required by applicable medical-records retention laws in the jurisdiction(s) where care is delivered. Where state law requires a longer period than HIPAA, the longer period applies. Several U.S. states require retention of adult medical records for at least 7 years from the date of last service, and longer for minors. Specific retention schedules are maintained internally and available on request.
  • Audit log entries are retained for at least 6 years from the date of creation or the date when last in effect, consistent with HIPAA § 164.316(b)(2).
  • Door verification photographs: retained as part of the visit record for the medical-records retention period above. We may de-identify or restrict access to these images sooner where doing so does not conflict with retention or legal-hold requirements.
  • Provider and operations account data is retained for the duration of the account and for a period thereafter as required by law, contractual obligation, or legitimate business need (including audit, dispute resolution, and tax recordkeeping).
  • Payment records are retained for the period required by applicable tax, accounting, and anti-money-laundering laws (typically at least 7 years in the United States).

When information is no longer required, we securely delete, destroy, or de-identify it in accordance with HHS guidance on disposal of PHI.

10. Legal Hold and Records Preservation

Notwithstanding any retention schedule described in this Policy, the Company may preserve records, audit logs, photographs, communications, and other information for longer periods where necessary to comply with legal hold requirements, litigation, governmental investigations, audits, regulatory inquiries, enforcement actions, contractual obligations, or other legal requirements. Information subject to a legal hold may be retained until the matter has been fully resolved, even if normal retention periods would otherwise permit deletion.

Such monitoring may include review of account activity, audit logs, check-in and check-out records, system access records, communications conducted through the Service, and other actions performed within the platform.

14. State-Specific Disclosures

14.1 California Residents (CCPA / CPRA / CMIA)

Personal information that is also PHI is exempt from CCPA/CPRA to the extent it is maintained in the same manner as PHI under HIPAA. For other personal information, California residents have the following rights:

  • Right to know what personal information we collect, use, disclose, and (if applicable) sell or share
  • Right to request deletion, subject to legal and medical-records retention exceptions
  • Right to correct inaccurate personal information
  • Right to limit use and disclosure of sensitive personal information
  • Right to non-discrimination for exercising privacy rights
  • Right to opt out of sale or sharing (we do not sell or share, as defined)

To exercise these rights, contact us at info@homerecoverypt.com. We will verify your identity before responding. You may also designate an authorized agent. Medical information also receives protection under California’s Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA).

14.2 Washington Residents (My Health My Data Act)

Washington residents whose “consumer health data” (as defined under the Washington My Health My Data Act) is processed through the Service have the right to confirm whether their consumer health data is being collected, used, or shared; to access it; to withdraw consent; and to request deletion. We collect and process consumer health data only with consent and only for the purposes described in this Policy. Contact us at info@homerecoverypt.com to exercise these rights.

14.3 Texas Residents (TX HB 300)

Texas’s Medical Records Privacy Act (HB 300) imposes obligations on entities that handle protected health information of Texas residents, including workforce training and breach response. We comply with HB 300 in addition to HIPAA where applicable.

14.4 Other U.S. States

Residents of Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, Oregon, Montana, and other states with comprehensive privacy laws may have similar rights to access, correct, delete, and obtain copies of their personal information. Contact us at info@homerecoverypt.com to exercise applicable state rights.

15. Children and Minors

The Service is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly permit children to create accounts or self-register. Where a minor is a patient:

  • The minor’s information is entered by a Service Coordinator with appropriate parental, guardian, or legal authorization, except where state law permits a minor to consent to treatment without parental involvement (for example, certain mental-health or reproductive-health services).
  • Access to the minor’s record is restricted in accordance with HIPAA and applicable state law on minor consent, parental access, and confidentiality of sensitive services.
  • We comply with the Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) by not knowingly collecting personal information directly from children under 13 through the Service.

If you believe we have inadvertently collected information directly from a child in violation of this Policy, contact us using the details in Section 20 and we will take prompt action.
